# Relatie regels 

1. If the behavior doesn't match the words, the words stop mattering. You don't believe promises, excuses, or explanations anymore. You only pay attention to repeated actions.

2. If communication becomes inconsistent, effort stops increasing. You don't text more. You don't try harder. You match the level that is being shown.

3. If plans are not clearly made, time stays protected. You don't hold space for "maybe." Your schedule stays open for people who actually confirm plans.

4. If someone hurts you and avoids accountability, trust stops growing. You don't keep rebuilding closeness while the same issue is ignored.

5. If conversations turn into blame or confusion, the discussion ends. You don't keep explaining yourself in circles.

6. If effort becomes one-sided, investment pauses. You stop planning, fixing, and maintaining the connection by yourself.

56

7. If saying no causes punishment, distance increases. Any relationship that punishes boundaries loses access to you.

8. If someone pulls away, pursuit stops. You allow space and watch whether they move toward you on their own.

9. If basic respect has to be explained, the dynamic changes. You stop trying to teach someone how to treat you.

10. If the same problem repeats after it's been discussed, the response changes. At that point the pattern is the answer.

11. If early red flags appear, attention goes to the pattern instead of the explanation.

12. If the relationship only works when you shrink yourself, participation stops.

13. If loyalty is not mutual, protection of the relationship ends.

14. Never put someone else's needs before your own. Always fill up your cup first. 

---

Assuming these are for dating/romantic relationships: overall, this is a solid self-respect/boundary framework—maybe 8/10 as a set of principles. Most rules are healthy correctives to over-functioning, people-pleasing, and self-abandonment. The main risk is using them as rigid unilateral rules/tests instead of communicated boundaries. Here’s each:

1. Actions > words — Strong. Repeated behavior is the most reliable data. Nuance: words still matter for communication/context; don’t become so guarded that you ignore normal human inconsistency.
2. Match inconsistent communication — Good as a protective move, but risky if done silently. Better: clarify once (“I’d like more consistent communication”), then match. Otherwise it can become tit-for-tat.
3. Don’t hold space for “maybe” — Healthy time-boundary. Nuance: people have lives; flexibility is fine. The issue is chronic vague non-commitment, not one busy week.
4. No accountability = no growing trust — Very strong. You can’t rebuild closeness while the hurt is denied/minimized. Nuance: accountability plus changed behavior can allow repair.
5. End circular blame/confusion conversations — Good. You don’t owe endless explaining. Nuance: don’t use it to avoid all conflict; the goal is to stop circular arguing, not to shut down legitimate discussion.
6. Pause one-sided investment — Strong. Stop being the only planner/fixer/maintainer. Nuance: communicate your need first; then observe.
7. Boundaries punished = less access — Excellent. A relationship that punishes “no” is unsafe or immature. This is one of the strongest rules.
8. Stop pursuing when someone pulls away — Generally healthy. Gives space and reveals reciprocal interest. Nuance: can become a game if overused; secure behavior is “give space, stay warm, don’t chase.”
9. Stop teaching basic respect — Strong. You can state expectations once, but you shouldn’t have to coach someone into basic decency. Nuance: some people genuinely need direct communication; the line is repeated teaching after clarity.
10. Repeated problem after discussion = pattern — Very strong. The pattern is the answer. Nuance: distinguish recurring unsolvable differences from active disregard; both matter, but they’re not identical.
11. Early red flags: watch the pattern, not the explanation — Good. Explanations are cheap; patterns are data. Nuance: avoid hypervigilance/trauma-based suspicion; red flags should be assessed over time.
12. Don’t shrink yourself to make it work — Excellent. If the relationship requires you to be smaller, less honest, or less yourself, that’s a major incompatibility.
13. Loyalty must be mutual — Good principle. Nuance: “protection of the relationship ends” can sound punitive/dramatic; better phrased as “stop over-protecting a one-sided relationship.”
14. Fill your cup first / never put their needs before yours — Half-right, but too absolute. Healthy relationships require mutual sacrifice and sometimes temporarily prioritizing a partner. The better rule is: don’t chronically self-abandon. “Never” is too rigid; interdependence matters.

Bottom line: Rules 1, 4, 6, 7, 10, 12, and 13 are especially strong. Rules 2, 3, 8, 9, and 11 are good with communication and context. Rules 5 and 14 need the most nuance. The healthiest use is as internal boundaries—“I won’t keep over-functioning”—not as silent tests or scorekeeping.
